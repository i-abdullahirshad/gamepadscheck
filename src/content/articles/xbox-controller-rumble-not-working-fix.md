## **Xbox Controller Rumble Not Working: 7 Fixes**

Rumble stopping is confusing because everything else usually keeps working. Buttons respond, sticks move, the controller connects normally, and only the vibration is missing. That pattern is actually useful information, because it rules out most connection problems straight away.

The cause is almost always one of three things: a setting switched off somewhere, a connection type that does not carry rumble properly, or software sitting between your controller and the game.

Work through these in order.

### **First, find out where the problem is**

Before changing anything, check whether the rumble motors themselves still work.

Open a gamepad tester with a vibration test and trigger it. If the controller vibrates there, the motors are fine and the problem sits in a game, a setting, or software. That narrows your search considerably.

If nothing happens in a tester either, focus on the connection and hardware fixes further down.

It is also worth checking rumble in more than one game before assuming the controller is at fault. Many games have their own vibration setting, and plenty of players discover it was switched off in only one title.

### **1 Check the game's own vibration setting**

This is the most common cause and the easiest to overlook.

Almost every game has a vibration toggle, usually under controller, gameplay or accessibility settings. Some games ship with it off by default. Some reset it after an update.

- Open the game's settings and look for vibration, rumble, haptics or force feedback  
- Check accessibility menus too, since vibration options are often placed there  
- If the game has separate sliders for strength, make sure they are not at zero

If rumble works in one game and not another, stop here. The problem is that game's settings, not your controller.

### **2 Check Windows and Xbox settings**

Windows and the Xbox app have their own vibration controls that override individual games.

- Open the Xbox Accessories app, select your controller, and check the vibration settings. This app lets you set strength for each motor separately, and a slider left at zero looks exactly like broken rumble  
- On an Xbox console, check Settings, then Accessibility, then Controller, and confirm vibration is enabled  
- Check Windows Settings under Accessibility, since some accessibility options reduce or disable vibration

The Xbox Accessories app is worth checking carefully. It has four separate strength controls covering the main motors and the trigger motors, and each one can be turned down independently.

### **3 Check your connection type**

This trips people up more than it should, because the controller works perfectly in every other way.

**Bluetooth is the usual problem.** Xbox controllers connected over Bluetooth support rumble, but support is inconsistent across games, and impulse triggers frequently do not work at all. Many games that vibrate fine over a cable will do nothing over Bluetooth.

Test this directly:

- Connect the controller with a USB cable and try the same game  
- If rumble returns, your Bluetooth connection was the cause

If it works wired but not wirelessly, your options are to play wired, or to use the official Xbox Wireless Adapter instead of Bluetooth. The adapter handles rumble far more reliably than Bluetooth does.

Also check your cable. A charge only cable delivers power but no data, so the controller lights up and works over Bluetooth while the cable does nothing. Use a cable you know transfers data.

### **4 Check battery level**

Low power affects rumble before it affects anything else.

Vibration motors draw far more current than buttons and sticks. When the battery is weak, a controller often disables or weakens rumble to keep the rest working. So you get a controller that responds normally and simply stops vibrating.

- Fit fresh batteries rather than testing the ones already inside  
- If you use a rechargeable pack, charge it fully and test again  
- Rechargeable packs lose capacity with age, and an old pack can show a healthy indicator while delivering too little current for the motors

If rumble returns with fresh batteries and fades again quickly, the pack is worn out.

### **5 Update the controller firmware**

Firmware updates fix vibration bugs, and out of date firmware causes odd behaviour that looks like hardware failure.

- Install the Xbox Accessories app from the Microsoft Store  
- Connect the controller with a data cable, since firmware updates are more reliable wired  
- Install any update the app offers, and test rumble in the app afterwards

If the app cannot detect your controller, revisit the cable and port before continuing.

### **6 Close software that intercepts the controller**

Programs that sit between your controller and your game frequently break rumble while leaving everything else intact.

- **DS4Windows and similar remapping tools** create a virtual controller. Vibration has to be passed through that virtual device, and it often is not. Close these completely and test again  
- **Steam Input** can cause the same problem. In Steam, right click the game, open Properties, then Controller, and try switching Steam Input on or off. Both directions fix it for different people  
- **Overlays and capture software** occasionally interfere as well

Test with everything closed. If rumble returns, reintroduce programs one at a time to find which one is responsible.

### **7 Reinstall the driver**

If rumble fails everywhere, including a gamepad tester, a broken driver install is a likely cause.

- Right click Start and open Device Manager  
- Find your controller under Xbox Peripherals, or under Human Interface Devices  
- Right click it, choose Uninstall device, then unplug the controller  
- Restart the PC and reconnect the controller so Windows reinstalls the driver

Also check Windows Update, since Xbox controller drivers arrive through it rather than a separate download. If rumble stopped right after a Windows update, try rolling the driver back in Device Manager instead.

### **When the motors have actually failed**

If rumble is dead in a gamepad tester, dead in every game, dead over both wired and wireless connections, and dead with fresh batteries, the hardware is the likely problem.

Signs that point to a physical fault:

- Only one side vibrates. Each grip has its own motor, so one working and one silent means a failed motor or a broken connection to it  
- Rumble is much weaker than it used to be, which suggests a motor wearing out  
- A rattling sound when you shake the controller, which can mean a motor weight has come loose  
- Rumble stopped immediately after a drop

Impulse triggers deserve a separate note. The small motors inside the triggers are distinct from the main grip motors, and they can fail on their own. If the grips vibrate but the triggers never do, check first that your game actually uses impulse triggers and that you are not on Bluetooth, since both explain it without any hardware fault.

For a controller under warranty, contact Xbox support before opening anything. Repairs are possible but require disassembly, and for an older controller the cost of a repair often approaches the cost of a replacement.

### **Quick reference**

- **Works in one game, not another:** that game's vibration setting  
- **Works wired, not wireless:** Bluetooth limitation. Use a cable or the wireless adapter  
- **Stopped suddenly, everything else fine:** battery level  
- **Nothing works anywhere including a tester:** driver, firmware, or hardware  
- **Grips vibrate but triggers do not:** impulse triggers, usually a Bluetooth or game support issue  
- **Only one grip vibrates:** a failed motor
